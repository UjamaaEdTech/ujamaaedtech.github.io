The Design Challenge

Build a progression, not a collection of computer lessons.

The curriculum organizes primary digital-technology learning across Basic 1–6 so skills develop progressively rather than appearing as disconnected topics.

The architecture moves from foundational computer familiarity into digital fluency, creation, responsible use, coding, multimedia, problem solving, and preparation for secondary learning.

INTRODUCE → BUILD FLUENCY → USE & NAVIGATE → CREATE & PARTICIPATE → CREATE WITH TECHNOLOGY → APPLY & SOLVE
Six-Level Architecture

Basic 1 through Basic 6.

BASIC 1

Introduce

Computer parts, safe start/shutdown, keyboard recognition, and simple typing.

BASIC 2

Build Fluency

Typing, mouse skills, simple programs, drawing, and digital tools in school and home contexts.

BASIC 3

Use & Navigate

Files, word processing, guided searching, and personal-information safety.

BASIC 4

Create & Participate

Document formatting, images, tables, multimedia, presentations, and digital citizenship.

BASIC 5

Create With Technology

Block-based coding, collaboration, research and validation, Scratch, and multimedia projects.

BASIC 6

Apply & Solve

Advanced multimedia, budgeting, Excel, an integrated project, and secondary-school preparation.

Curriculum Threads

Four threads run across the pathway.

Digital Fluency

Progressive ability to operate and use digital tools.

Responsible Use

Safety, personal information awareness, copyright, and ethical behavior.

Creation

Movement from using tools to producing documents, media, presentations, and projects.

Problem Solving

Application of digital tools, coding, data, and projects to practical challenges.

Learning Experiences

Practical activities anchor the curriculum.
